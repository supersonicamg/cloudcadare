What this version is built on

  1. Say what you offer in the hero. The homepage must state the value proposition in a plain tagline (NN/g, homepage design principles). So the H1 names the thing: a live online course in cloud, DevOps and FinOps. The brand name and "CEAP" come second.
  2. Real people get looked at, stock people get skipped. Users ignore decorative feel-good images and study photos that carry information, like real people (NN/g, Photos as web content). So stock photos are only stand-ins, and the 15 real placements carry the proof.
  3. Visible navigation beats a hamburger. Hidden navigation is less discoverable on phones and desktops; with 4–5 items you can keep them visible (NN/g, Hamburger menus, mobile study). So phones get a visible 4-link row, no menu button.
  4. Carousels and swipe rows get skipped. You can't count on people seeing carousel content (NN/g, Mobile carousels; Adrian Roselli). So placements stay a grid on phones, with "show all".
  5. A sticky button only after the main one scrolls away, and slim. Tall or animated bars feel like pressure (sticky CTA guidance; mixed A/B results at Clean Commit). So the phone bar appears after the hero button is gone and hides at the batch section.
  6. Thumbs and targets. People hold phones in three grips and reach the middle best (Smashing, Thumb zone (Hoober)). So main actions are full width, every target is at least 44px.
  7. Few sizes, clear jumps. A type scale should use a few sizes with clear relationships (Google Fonts, Scale); size alone is weak hierarchy, so combine it with weight and colour (EightShapes). So: one family, two weights, seven text roles. Turn on "Show type scale" to see them.
  8. Components respond to their own width. Intrinsic grids and fluid type do most of the work; container queries handle real structural changes (Frontend Masters, LogRocket). So every section here adapts by its own width, not by "phone" or "tablet" breakpoints.

      Questions before you join

      Do I need an IT background?

      No. Month 1 starts from cloud basics. If you are completely new to IT, a counsellor will suggest a short warm-up first.

      How many hours a week?

      About 8 to 10: two or three live evening classes, plus labs in your own time.

      What if I miss a class?

      Every class is recorded and up the same night, and your mentor helps you catch up on the lab.

      Can I pay in instalments?

      Yes. Pay by UPI, card or net banking, in one go or monthly.

      Do I get a certificate?

      Yes, a CEAP certificate employers can verify online. AWS, Azure and Google Cloud exam fees are paid to the exam provider.

      Do you guarantee a job?

      No, and be wary of anyone who does. We give you the skills, a capstone, mock interviews, CV and LinkedIn help, and introductions.
